Grand Button Incremental

Grand Button Incremental is basically just your typical button simulator game on Roblox, where you press buttons, open runes, ascend for Angels and a lot more.

We're going to focus on the notation this game uses, as it is rather interesting. It starts off with your usual prefixes: k, M, B, T, q, Q, s, S, o and n, the ones place prefixes are U, D, t, q, Q, s, S, o and n. After a decillion though, the prefixes are designed to fit in 2 characters: the tens place prefixes are d, Vg, Tg, qg, Qg, sg, Sg, Og and Ng; their one-character equivalents are d, V, T, q, Q, s, S, O and N (these are used if the -illion number is not divisible by 10, like qS (74th illion)) and something that I call "special prefixes": they are d, V, T, qg, Qg, sg, Sg, O and N (these are only used if the -illion number is divisible by 10 and is above 100, like VCe (120) and qgtc (340)). The hundreds place prefixes are Cen, DuC, TrC, qGe, QGe, sCe, SCe, OGe and NGe, their two-character equivalents are Ce, Dc, tc, qG, QG, sc, Sc, OG and NG (used if the -illion number mod 100 is less than 10, 108 would be oCe) and their one-character equivalents are C, D, t, q, Q, s, S, O and N.

The 1,000th -illion is given the prefix MilN... and it stops here. Unfortunately, this notation has ambiguities at -illion 106th and 600th (both sCe) and -illion 107th and 700th (both SCe).

Denutation

Denutation is basically Standard, but each -illion is 10x larger than the previous. The first few prefixes are: K (thousand), M (million), B (billion, 2), T (tres, 3), Q (quattuor, 4), P (pente, 5), H (hexi, 6), S (septem, 7), O (octo, 8) and N (novem); the ones place prefixes are O (one), B, T, Q, F, S, H, E (eight) and N; the tens place prefixes are derived from English number names: Te, Tw, Th, Fo, Fi, Si, Se, Et and Nt; and the hundreds place prefixes use mixed Latin and English prefixes: C (centum, 100), DC (ducenti, 200), TC (trecenti, 300), QC (quadrigenti, 400), FH (five hundred), SC (sescenti, 600), SH (seven hundred), EH (eight hundred), NH (nine hundred). The prefix order is hundreds - tens - ones.

Unlike regular Standard variations, there are no prefixes for multiples of 1,000 -illions and are instead repersented by dashes (1,000 would be O-K, the K is used as 0) and can theortically go on forever, but Bad Notations choose to stop abbreviating after 101.797 x 10308 to prevent strings from getting too long.

fBAU's standard

fBAU's standard is a weird variation of Standard, with the most notable part being the prefix is placed BEFORE the number. The prefixes start out like a typical Standard variant, but they're somewhat warped: the first few prefixes are k, Ml, Bl, Tl, qu, Qu, se, Se, Ot and No; the ones place prefixes are Ue, Do, Tc, qu, Qu, se, Se, Ot, and No; the tens place prefixes are Dc, Vt, Ti, qi, Qi, si, Si, Oi and Ni; and the hundreds place prefixes are Cn, Du, Tr, qr, Qr, sl, Sl, Ol and Nn. Unlike typical Standard variations, this variation has prefixes for every power of 10: the thousands place prefixes are Mil, Mic, Nan, Pic, Fem, Att, Zep, Yoc, Xon and Vec; the ten thousands place prefixes are Mec, Mei, Tri, Tet, Pen, Hex, Hep, Oct and Enn; the hundred thousands place prefixes are Hec, Doh, Tir, Tea, Pea, Hea, Hpa, Oca and Ena (note that Vec is only used for 10 thousand, 110 thousand, 210 thousand and so on); the millions place prefixes are Kil, Dki, Tki, Qak, Qik, Sxk, Spk, Oki and Nki; the ten millions place prefixes are Vee, Vve, Tve, Qav, Qiv, Sxv, Spv, Ove and Nve; the hundred millions place prefixes are Het, Dhe, The, Qah, Qih, Sxh, Sph, Ohe, Nhe; and the 1 billionth -illion is given the prefix Meg, which is where the system ends. The prefix order is billions - 1 - 10 - 100 millions - 1 - 10 - 100 thousands - 1 - 10 - 100.
